How to Fix Slow Phone Charging (8 Quick Fixes)

Plugging in your phone and watching the percentage barely move is frustrating. The cause is usually something simple and cheap to fix: a worn cable, a weak charger, or a port full of lint. Work through these fixes in order, starting with the easiest.

1. Try a different cable

Cables wear out, especially near the connectors, and a damaged one can charge slowly or on and off. Try another cable you trust. Some cheap cables are built for data or only support low power, so they can't charge fast even when they look fine.

2. Check your charger's power

Old or tiny chargers, like the 5-watt cubes that came with older phones, charge much slower than modern ones. Look at the small print on the charger for its output in watts (W). Many current phones charge faster with a charger of 20W or more. Check your phone's specs to see what it supports, because a stronger charger won't make a phone charge faster than its own limit.

3. Plug into the wall, not a computer or weak port

A laptop's USB port, a car's older USB socket, or a cheap multi-port hub often gives much less power than a wall charger. For the fastest charging, plug a proper charger straight into a wall outlet.

4. Clean the charging port

Pocket lint and dust pack into the port over time and can stop the cable from sitting fully. Turn the phone off first. Then gently scrape the port with a wooden or plastic toothpick, or blow it out with a short burst of air. Never use metal objects like pins or paper clips, because they can damage the contacts.

5. Stop using the phone while it charges

Gaming, video, navigation, and hotspot use all draw power while you charge, so the battery fills slowly or even drains. Put the phone down, or turn on airplane mode for a quick top-up.

6. Take off the case and keep it cool

Heat slows charging on purpose, because your phone limits its speed to protect the battery. A thick case, direct sun, or a hot car all trap heat. Remove the case, move to a cooler spot, and avoid charging under a pillow or blanket.

7. Restart and update your phone

A software glitch can slow charging. Restart the phone, then check for updates in your settings. Updates often fix battery and charging bugs. If you recently installed a new app and charging got worse, uninstall it to see if it helps.

8. Check the battery's health

An old battery can charge slowly, drain fast, or shut off early. On iPhone, open Settings → Battery → Battery Health. Many Android phones show similar information in Settings or the maker's care app. If health is low, a battery replacement from the maker or a trusted repair shop is usually much cheaper than a new phone.

Wireless charging is slower

If you charge wirelessly, expect it to be slower than a cable. It also makes more heat, and it works best when the phone sits centered on the pad. For a quick top-up, use a cable.

A note on cheap chargers

Buying a budget charger or cable is fine, but avoid the very cheapest unbranded ones. Poorly made chargers can overheat or damage your battery. Look for well-reviewed products from known brands, and for safety certifications noted on the product page.

Quick recap

Swap the cable, check the charger's wattage, use a wall outlet, clean the port, stop using the phone while it charges, keep it cool, restart and update, and check battery health. If none of that helps, the battery or the phone's charging hardware may need repair.
